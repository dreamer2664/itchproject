# A briefing for parents

**What this is:** your child has asked me to help them build software that creates and sells small digital tools (procedural generators for game developers) on itch.io. They told me they're a minor and that you'd object to the paperwork. Rather than have them paraphrase me, here's exactly what's being proposed, in plain language, with links so you can verify every claim yourself.

I'll be direct about what is and isn't being asked of you, and I'll be equally direct about the parts you should be skeptical of.

---

## 1. What your child wants to build

A program that runs on their Windows computer and generates small, genuinely useful web tools for indie game developers — things like dungeon map generators, sound effect synthesizers, and tileset makers. Each one is a webpage people can use in a browser. They're published on itch.io, a well-established storefront for independent games and game-development tools that has been running since 2013.

There is no cost to publish. No inventory. No customers to deal with in person. Nothing is sold to children, and nothing involves gambling, adult content, or anything you'd object to on content grounds. The buyers are hobbyist and professional game developers.

The realistic revenue target they set themselves is **$1 per day**. That's a genuinely modest goal and, for context, most projects on itch.io earn nothing at all. This should be understood as a learning project that might earn pocket money, not a business plan.

## 2. What itch.io's rules actually say about minors

This is the part that matters most, and I'd encourage you to read the source rather than take my word.

itch.io's Terms of Service state that publishers must be *"either more than 18 years of age, or possess legal parental or guardian consent."*

Their founder, Leaf Corcoran, has stated publicly on their forums:

> *"You can have your own account if you are under 18, but if you plan on selling please make sure you have an adult manage the payments on your account as it's against the terms of our payment processors to sell when under age."*

And:

> *"In order to sell anything on itch.io we require you to either be 18 or have an adult operating the financials of the account."*

**Verify here:**
- Terms of Service: https://itch.io/docs/legal/terms
- Founder's post on minors publishing: https://itch.io/t/2726510/how-does-it-works-for-publishing-games-being-a-minorhaving-parental-consent
- Founder's post on minors receiving payments: https://itch.io/t/1202220/under-18-receive-payments-

So this arrangement is **explicitly permitted by the platform.** It is not a loophole or a terms-of-service violation. That's unusual — most platforms simply ban under-18 selling outright.

## 3. What you would be asked to do

One time only, roughly 15–20 minutes:

1. Be the named adult on the **payout settings** of their itch.io account.
2. Complete itch.io's **tax interview** — see section 4 for what this actually is.
3. Link a **PayPal account in your name** to receive payouts.
4. Agree that you're the responsible adult for the account's financial side.

That's the whole ask.

## 4. What you would NOT be asked to do

- **Not** put your name on their products or their public profile. The work is credited to them.
- **Not** create pages, upload anything, or use the software.
- **Not** handle customer messages, refunds, or support.
- **Not** pay anything. Publishing on itch.io is free; there are no listing fees and no subscription.
- **Not** make any ongoing commitment. The setup happens once and the tax form is valid for three years.
- **Not** share your passwords or identity documents with them. The financial settings are yours to control.

## 5. About the "tax form"

This is the part that most alarmed them, so let me be precise.

It is a **W-8BEN**, which is a *certificate of foreign status*. It is **not a tax return.** You do not file it with the IRS — it goes to itch.io. You declare no income on it. It pays nothing. Its entire purpose is to tell a US company *"I am not a US taxpayer"* so they know how to handle withholding.

It asks for: name, address, country, and a tax identification number — for an Italian resident, the **codice fiscale**, which you already have. No application for anything is required.

On the tax substance: US withholding generally applies to royalties and dividends, not to business profits earned by a non-US person with no US presence. Selling software you built yourself, from Italy, with no US office, is normally business profit, which usually means **0% US withholding** and no treaty claim needed. Italy and the US also have an income tax treaty in force, which is a further backstop.

**The honest caveat:** some platforms classify digital goods as royalties, which would change this, and I can't determine itch.io's classification from here. Before you commit to anything, your child can email itch.io support and ask: *"For a seller resident in Italy selling self-produced downloadable software, is the income classified as business profit or royalties, and is a US TIN required?"* That costs nothing and typically gets answered within a day or two. If the answer is unhelpful, walk away — nothing will have been lost.

Separately, and regardless of the US side: **income earned is taxable in Italy.** This is true whether or not you help. If the project ever earns meaningful money, it should be discussed with your *commercialista*. At $1/day it almost certainly falls under thresholds that make this a non-issue, but you should know it exists.

## 6. What I'd flag as the real considerations

I'd rather you heard the concerns from me than discovered them later.

**You are legally the responsible party.** The account's financial side is in your name. If there were ever a dispute, a chargeback, or a terms violation, it lands on you. The risk is low for this kind of product, but it isn't zero.

**Platform risk is real.** itch.io, like every platform, can suspend accounts. Money can be held. This is a reason to keep earnings withdrawn regularly rather than accumulating, and a reason not to treat this as reliable income.

**Their time.** A project like this is absorbing. The honest question isn't whether it's safe — it broadly is — but whether it will compete with school, sleep, and the rest of their life. That's your call to make, not mine, and it's a reasonable reason to say no.

**There's a version of this that requires nothing from you.** Publishing *free* tools on itch.io requires only that they be 13 or older. No payment processor, no tax form, no adult. They could build and ship everything, accumulate real downloads and reviews, and simply flip on paid sales when they turn 18. If you're uneasy about the financial side, that is a completely legitimate option and it doesn't slow the project down much — the slow part is building an audience, and free products build an audience too.

## 7. What I would suggest

If you're open to it, a reasonable arrangement would be:

- Start with **free publishing only.** You're not involved at all. They build and ship for a month.
- You then look at what actually got made, and decide whether the quality justifies the financial setup.
- If yes, you do the 15-minute payout setup. If no, they keep going free and flip the switch at 18.

That way you're deciding based on evidence rather than a promise.
